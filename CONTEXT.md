## 1. Purpose of This File

Use this file as the shared context for all work on Stix N Vibes.

Read this file before you design, code, refactor, write product copy, or
change the website.

Your goal is not to make a generic online shop. Your goal is to build a
distinctive, premium, culture-led sticker and print brand. The site must
feel art-directed, tactile, confident, and memorable. It must also be
usable, fast, accessible, and commercially clear.

The target is an award-level digital experience. Treat that as a quality
bar, not as a claim that an award has been won.

**Core brand line:** `YOUR STICKERS. YOUR VIBE.`\
**Supporting campaign line:** `MAKE IT STICK. LITERALLY.`

The second line connects the physical act of sticking something to the
emotional act of making an idea, identity, or memory last.

## 3. Brand Overview

Stix N Vibes is a youth-oriented sticker, art-print, and personalised
physical-goods brand. It helps people express identity, taste, humour,
memories, communities, and creative ideas through stickers and printed
objects.

The brand serves individual customers and organisations. Customers may
buy a ready-made sticker pack, choose a print or wall-decor product,
personalise a design, commission custom stickers, or ask about branded
and collaborative work.

The experience should combine:

-   The energy of sticker culture and street graphics.
-   The finish and restraint of a premium design studio.
-   The tactile feel of real printed objects.
-   The confidence of a modern direct-to-consumer brand.
-   The flexibility of personalisation.
-   Clear product information and a trustworthy buying journey.

Do not position the brand as only a sticker shop. Stickers are the core
of the identity, but the product world also includes framed and unframed
wall decor, poster prints, cards, music-inspired collectible cards,
creator collaborations, and custom print work.

### 3.1 Brand promise

**Make self-expression physical.** Turn a personal vibe, an inside joke,
an artwork, a memory, a music obsession, or a brand identity into
something people can hold, display, gift, or stick somewhere meaningful.

### 3.2 Brand personality

The brand is:

-   Bold, not noisy.
-   Playful, not childish.
-   Premium, not precious.
-   Youthful, not disposable.
-   Experimental, not confusing.
-   Direct, not pushy.
-   Expressive, not generic.
-   Tactile, not sterile.
-   Inclusive, not exclusive.

### 3.3 Audience

Design for these overlapping groups:

1.  **Self-expression buyers:** people who personalise laptops, bottles,
    notebooks, phone cases, helmets, desks, and everyday objects.
2.  **Students and young creatives:** people who use stickers, prints,
    and cards to show their interests and decorate their spaces.
3.  **Music and culture fans:** people who enjoy album-inspired
    collectible cards, playlist aesthetics, artist drops, and music-led
    decor.
4.  **Room and desk decorators:** people who want A4/A3 posters, sticker
    posters, cards, and framed pieces for bedrooms, studios, dorms, and
    workspaces.
5.  **Gift buyers:** people who want something personal, visual,
    affordable, and easy to give.
6.  **Artists and creators:** people who want to release designs, build
    an audience, or make collaborative products.
7.  **Brands and small businesses:** cafés, events, bands, creators,
    shops, and other organisations that need branded stickers or printed
    goods.

Do not force every audience into one generic message. Use clear paths by
need: shop, decorate, customise, collaborate, or order for a brand.

## 5. Brand Voice and Copy Rules

Use direct, short, clear English. Follow the principles of ASD-STE100:

-   Use one main idea per sentence.
-   Prefer common words.
-   Use active voice.
-   Give clear instructions.
-   Keep paragraphs short.
-   Explain specialist terms when needed.
-   Avoid vague claims.
-   Avoid unnecessary hype.
-   Use consistent names for the same product or action.

The brand may use informal phrases such as "YOUR VIBE," "MAKE IT STICK,"
and "STICKERS SHOULD SAY SOMETHING." Keep supporting information clear
and accurate.

### Good copy direction

-   "Make your space feel like yours."
-   "Pick a pack. Set your vibe."
-   "Turn your artwork into stickers."
-   "Build a wall that feels like you."
-   "Make a card for the song that means something."
-   "Custom stickers for your next drop."
-   "Make it stick. Literally."

These are copy directions, not claims about a feature or a product
specification.

### Avoid

-   Empty claims such as "the best quality in the world."
-   Fake urgency such as "only 2 left" when stock data does not confirm
    it.
-   Fake reviews or unverified customer counts.
-   Long paragraphs that bury product details.
-   Generic corporate language.
-   Too many exclamation marks.
-   Copy that treats every product as a sticker pack.
-   Unclear wording about frames, poster formats, card formats, or
    customisation.

## 6. Visual Direction: Lemon, Black, Ice, and 3D

The current visual direction is a strict black-and-lemon system with a
controlled ice/frost treatment and tactile 3D product presentation.

This direction overrides older PRD examples that suggest blue/purple,
pink, or orange palettes.

### 6.1 Core palette

Use these as the design tokens unless the existing design system has a
documented equivalent:

-   **Near black:** `#050505` or `#090909` --- main background.
-   **Lemon:** `#D7FF3F` --- key accent, active state, important CTA,
    selected detail.
-   **Warm off-white:** `#F5F5EF` --- primary text on dark surfaces.
-   **Muted grey:** `#9A9A94` --- secondary text.
-   **Dark surface:** `#151515` --- raised panels and cards.
-   **Border:** `#292929` --- fine rules and card boundaries.
-   **Ice highlight:** cool near-white with low opacity --- reflections,
    frost, and glass edge detail only.

Keep lemon as the recognisable accent. Do not let blue or cyan become a
competing brand colour. Cool highlights may appear in the ice effect,
but the page must still read as black and lemon at first glance.

Check text contrast. Do not place small lemon text on a bright ice
highlight or low-contrast glass surface.

### 6.2 Reference direction: 8B Sill

Use the supplied reference at `https://8b.com/templates/sill/demo.html`
as a **design and interaction reference**, not as a business-content
template.

Preserve the qualities that make a designed template feel intentional:
clear section rhythm, strong image-led moments, purposeful transitions,
good spacing, and a coherent story as the user scrolls.

Translate those qualities into the Stix N Vibes product world. Do not
copy pet-boarding content, imagery, names, prices, or business claims.
Do not clone the template mechanically. The current product scope,
black-and-lemon identity, and this file take priority.

Before implementation, inspect the reference in a browser if available.
Record the actual layout and motion that you intend to adapt. If the
demo cannot be reached, state that limitation and use only verified
template-page information plus this brief. Do not claim that you
inspected details that you did not see.

### 6.3 Ice and frost effect

The ice/frost effect should make selected elements feel cold, glossy,
dimensional, and physical. It must not turn the entire website into a
blue glassmorphism template.

Use ice selectively:

-   On a hero object or a small number of product display surfaces.
-   As a translucent edge, glossy reflection, or frosted panel.
-   In a limited number of hover and scroll transitions.
-   As a subtle visual contrast against black and lemon.
-   Around 3D objects where light can define depth.

Good ice treatments include realistic refraction, fine frost near the
edges, a soft inner highlight, a restrained specular streak, and layered
translucent material. Keep text areas readable.

Avoid:

-   A frosted-glass panel behind every card.
-   Strong blue glow across the whole page.
-   Thick blur that hides product artwork.
-   Excessive bloom, particles, fog, or lens flares.
-   Frost that makes a product look damaged or difficult to understand.
-   Effects that reduce contrast or cause expensive rendering on mobile.

### 6.4 3D product direction

Use 3D to make physical products feel tangible. Examples include:

-   A die-cut sticker with a visible edge, slight curl, and soft contact
    shadow.
-   A sticker sheet floating above a dark studio surface.
-   An A4/A3 poster or framed print shown with believable depth.
-   A card stack with paper edges and controlled reflections.
-   A music-inspired card rotating slowly in a studio light.
-   A small cluster of stickers layered at different distances from the
    camera.

The product artwork must remain the focus. Use real product images and
approved artwork whenever available.

**Do not redraw, rewrite, recolour, crop away, distort, or replace
supplied sticker artwork without explicit approval.** Keep original
artwork as the source of truth. Add lighting, shadow, depth, rotation,
and environment around it.

Do not use generic 3D laptop, bottle, helmet, or phone models merely to
fill space. Show a device only when it helps explain how the product is
used. If a mockup changes the shape, colour, or legibility of the
sticker, choose a simpler presentation.

### 6.5 Composition and negative space

-   Use large, confident type.
-   Use a clear grid and deliberate asymmetry.
-   Let product imagery create the visual energy.
-   Leave enough empty space around the headline and CTA.
-   Keep each section focused on one message.
-   Use overlapping objects with clear depth order.
-   Keep the main product recognisable at a glance.
-   Do not fill every empty area with a sticker.
-   Avoid random floating objects with no story.
-   Do not make every section a hero section.

The site should feel art-directed, not crowded.

### 7.2 Hero principles

The hero must look heavy but implement light.

Recommended architecture:

-   Semantic HTML for the headline and supporting copy.
-   HTML links and buttons for the calls to action.
-   A poster or still image that can render immediately.
-   Separate product/sticker layers when they add real value.
-   Optional video or scroll-scrubbed media that is not required for
    basic function.
-   A static fallback for reduced motion, failed media, or unsupported
    devices.

The hero must not depend on a video to communicate the brand name,
headline, or CTA.

Use motion to reveal the product and build a sense of depth. Do not make
the visitor wait for a long intro. Keep the main message visible and
readable.

Use the customer's actual supplied sticker images. Do not invent
replacement sticker artwork to make the hero look complete.

### 7.3 Product detail pages

Every product detail page must answer, as applicable:

-   What is it?
-   What is included?
-   What size or format is it?
-   What can the customer customise?
-   What is the price?
-   Is it in stock or made to order?
-   How does it ship?
-   What material or finish is used?
-   What is the next action?

Show price and availability from the real product data. Do not hard-code
a price in multiple components if the application has a central product

### 7.4 Customisation journey

Keep the steps understandable:

1.  Choose the product or custom order type.
2.  Upload artwork or choose a supported template.
3.  Enter the supported personalisation fields.

5.  Review size, price, and production details.

Show validation messages near the relevant control. Keep the user's work

Do not pretend that a preview is a print-ready proof unless it has been
designed and validated for that purpose.

### 7.5 B2B journey

Use a clear, professional path for brands and businesses. Ask only for
information needed to respond to the enquiry. State what happens after
submission. Confirm that the form really sends data before claiming that
an enquiry has been received.

## 8. Motion and Interaction Principles

Motion must support hierarchy, tactility, and navigation. It must not be
decoration without purpose.

Use motion for:

-   Gentle sticker float or parallax.
-   Short peel, lift, or tilt effects on product hover.
-   Scroll reveals that guide the eye.
-   A controlled transition between product sections.
-   A featured product that gains scale or depth as it enters view.
-   Subtle reflection or lighting changes on selected 3D elements.

Rules:

-   Prefer short, controlled transitions.
-   Use the PRD's 300 ms target as a general guide for common UI
    transitions, not as a hard limit for every cinematic sequence.
-   Avoid scroll hijacking.
-   Do not trap the user in pinned sections for too long.
-   Do not make the page hard to scroll or use.
-   Do not animate all elements at once.
-   Keep important content visible if JavaScript or animation fails.
-   Support `prefers-reduced-motion`.
-   Provide a complete static experience when reduced motion is enabled.
-   Pause or simplify continuous animation when the user is not
    interacting or when the page is hidden, where appropriate.
-   Avoid flashing effects.
-   Do not use motion to hide loading delays or missing content.

## 9. Technical and Repository Rules

The current project context indicates a React, TypeScript, Vite, and
Tailwind-based application with existing routes and modular components.
Verify the repository before relying on this statement. Do not migrate
the framework simply because a reference template uses one HTML file.

### 9.1 Inspect before editing

Before every significant task:

1.  Read this file.
2.  Inspect the relevant components, routes, data, and assets.
3.  Check the package scripts and installed dependencies.
4.  Review `git status` and the diff.
5.  Identify the existing behavior that must remain.
6.  Make a small, targeted plan.
7.  Implement the change.
8.  Run the relevant checks.
9.  Review the final diff.

Do not overwrite unrelated work. Do not reset, clean, or revert files
that the user has changed unless the user explicitly asks.

### 9.2 Preserve existing functionality

Protect all working features. These may include:

-   Route navigation and not-found behavior.
-   Product and collection data.
-   Search and filtering.
-   Cart state, quantity changes, pricing, and checkout entry points.
-   Customiser upload, templates, preview, and validation.
-   Forms, enquiries, and WhatsApp links.
-   Account and order pages.
-   FAQ and support content.
-   Analytics events.
-   SEO metadata, sitemap, robots rules, and structured data.
-   Responsive layouts and accessibility behavior.

Inspect the current repository to establish which of these features
exist. This list is a preservation checklist, not proof that every
feature is complete.

Do not replace a working multi-page React application with a single
static HTML file just to match the reference template.

### 9.3 Components and design tokens

-   Reuse the current component system when practical.
-   Keep product data separate from presentation.
-   Reuse the existing design tokens or consolidate duplicates
    carefully.
-   Avoid repeated magic values for colours, spacing, radii, and
    animation timing.
-   Keep components focused.
-   Do not add a dependency for an effect that CSS or the current stack
    can handle well.
-   Do not add a large 3D library unless the visual result requires it
    and performance is justified.
-   Prefer CSS transforms, static renders, and optimised video for
    simple depth effects.
-   Use WebGL or a 3D engine only when it produces clear value.

### 9.4 Assets and file paths

-   Verify that every referenced asset exists.
-   Use consistent, descriptive file names.
-   Use the correct image format and responsive sizes.
-   Add dimensions or aspect ratios to reduce layout shift.
-   Provide useful alt text for meaningful images.
-   Use empty alt text for purely decorative images.
-   Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
-   Prioritise the main hero poster when it is the LCP image.
-   Do not lazy-load the main image if that would delay the first view.
-   Do not preload large video files by default.
-   Ensure the mobile layout uses appropriately sized media.
-   Keep a visible fallback if media fails.
-   Do not ship temporary procedural artwork as if it were final brand
    artwork.
-   Mark placeholder and final assets clearly in code or asset
    documentation.

### 9.5 Video and 3D performance

Use the simplest technique that achieves the intended art direction.

-   Load a poster before optional video.
-   Keep headline and CTA as HTML.
-   Defer optional video when appropriate.
-   Use `preload="metadata"` or a more conservative setting when
    justified.
-   Verify the actual network requests for desktop and mobile variants.
-   Confirm that the browser downloads the intended media variant.
-   Remove or disable failed media without breaking the hero.
-   Use static imagery for reduced motion.
-   Avoid multiple large, simultaneous videos.
-   Compress images and video.
-   Measure actual mobile performance. Do not infer it from asset size
    alone.

### 9.6 Accessibility

Aim for WCAG 2.1 AA.

-   Use semantic headings and landmarks.
-   Use one clear main page heading.
-   Give every interactive control an accessible name.
-   Keep keyboard focus visible.
-   Ensure logical tab order.
-   Make menus, dialogs, accordions, filters, and forms work with a
    keyboard.
-   Ensure text contrast meets the relevant requirements.
-   Do not communicate status with colour alone.
-   Give meaningful product images descriptive alt text.
-   Hide decorative 3D layers from assistive technology.
-   Respect reduced-motion preferences.
-   Ensure sticky and animated elements do not cover focus or content.
-   Test actual keyboard interaction. A DOM scan alone is not an
    accessibility audit.

### 9.7 SEO and trust

-   Use unique page titles and descriptions.
-   Use semantic HTML and descriptive links.
-   Add Open Graph metadata.
-   Keep sitemap and robots rules correct.
-   Use structured product data only when it matches visible, verified
    product information.
-   Do not add fake reviews or ratings to schema.
-   Make product, shipping, return, and contact information easy to
    find.
-   Do not display payment or security badges unless the service is
    actually used.
-   Do not claim SSL, PCI, or payment-provider integration beyond what
    is verified.

### 9.8 Performance and quality targets

Use these targets from the PRD as goals:

-   Lighthouse Performance: at least 80 on mobile.
-   Accessibility: at least 90.
-   SEO: at least 90.
-   Best Practices: at least 90.
-   Time to Interactive: under 5 seconds where measured by the chosen
    test method.
-   Aim for a fast first view on mobile networks.

These are targets, not verified results. Report the test device,
environment, and result. Do not claim a score if Lighthouse or an
equivalent test was not run.

## 10. Commerce, Pricing, and Content Integrity

Trust is part of the design.

-   Show prices in the configured currency and use one source of truth.
-   The PRD proposes INR as the primary currency. Confirm the current
    store configuration before displaying currency labels or
    conversions.
-   Show taxes, shipping, and bulk terms according to the actual
    business rules.
-   Do not promise waterproof, weatherproof, UV-resistant,
    scratch-resistant, or outdoor durability unless the specification
    confirms it.
-   Do not invent dispatch or delivery dates.
-   Do not show fake stock levels or countdowns.
-   Do not show fake reviews, follower counts, sold counts, or client
    logos.
-   Do not invent customer quotes.
-   Use accurate product photos. If a render is illustrative, label it
    where needed.
-   Keep returns, privacy, terms, and shipping information accessible.
-   Make custom-order expectations clear before the customer submits a
    request.

## 11. Copyright, Brand Safety, and Customer Artwork

Stix N Vibes may work with user-submitted artwork, artist work,
music-related references, and brand logos. Handle these carefully.

-   Do not use a third-party logo or artist artwork as an official
    collaboration without permission.
-   Do not suggest endorsement by Spotify or a music artist unless it is
    confirmed.
-   Do not remove artist credits or watermarks from supplied assets.
-   Do not edit customer artwork beyond the requested task.
-   Do not train or generate substitute artwork that imitates a supplied
    customer's design unless the user explicitly requests an allowed
    transformation.
-   Keep original files unchanged. Create derivatives as separate files.
-   Do not publish user-uploaded artwork or photos without the required
    permission.
-   If ownership or commercial-use rights are unclear, flag the issue
    before publishing.

## 12. Content and Asset Production Workflow

Use this sequence when new visuals are required:

1.  Confirm the product and the intended use.
2.  Locate the real artwork or product image.
3.  Preserve the source asset.
4.  Create a still composition first when the layout or artwork is not
    yet approved.
5.  Review the still at desktop and mobile sizes.
6.  Add depth, light, shadow, and ice effects without changing the
    source artwork.
7.  Animate only after the composition works as a still.
8.  Export an optimised poster and video if video is needed.
9.  Integrate the assets into the real components.
10. Test fallback, reduced motion, and mobile loading.

Use actual Stix N Vibes artwork as the source of truth. AI-generated
visuals may support the environment or concept, but must not invent
official product art, logos, product specifications, or collaborator
identities.

## 13. Recommended Art Direction by Product

### Stickers

Use close-up photography or renders with die-cut edges, subtle
thickness, a soft contact shadow, and controlled surface reflection.
Keep the graphic readable. Show the product on a real surface when
useful.

### Sticker packs

Show the pack as a curated set. Use a clear hero product image and
smaller details. Avoid scattering so many stickers that customers cannot
understand what they will receive.

### A4/A3 sticker posters

Show the full sheet or poster first. Add close-up crops for texture and
details. Make the format and whether it is peelable or printed clear.

### Framez

Show the actual framed or unframed format. Use believable room scale and
simple surroundings. Let the art lead. Avoid over-staging the room so
the product becomes secondary.

### Cards

Use paper edges, stacked arrangements, tactile shadows, and a clear view
of the front design. Show the reverse only if it is relevant and
accurate.

### Music cards

Use a personal, collectible presentation. Make custom fields legible.
Use approved images and do not imply official affiliation.

### Custom and B2B products

Use real examples and clear process information. Visual effects should
support trust, not make the ordering requirements harder to understand.

## 14. Testing and Definition of Done

A task is not complete because the page looks good in one screenshot.

Run the checks that apply to the change:

-   Build and lint.
-   Route smoke tests.
-   Product and pricing checks.
-   Asset-path checks.
-   Console and network error checks.
-   Desktop, tablet, and mobile review.
-   Keyboard navigation.
-   Reduced-motion review.
-   Form validation and submission checks.
-   Cart and customiser regression tests.
-   SEO metadata and structured-data checks.
-   Performance tests when performance is affected.

For major visual changes, review at minimum:

-   1440 × 900
-   1024 × 768
-   768 × 1024
-   390 × 844

Also test at a real phone size when possible.

Check:

-   The first view is clear and visually distinctive.
-   The product artwork is recognisable.
-   Text and CTAs do not collide with 3D elements.
-   No horizontal overflow appears.
-   The page remains usable if media fails.
-   Reduced motion gives a complete experience.
-   All important controls work.
-   No unrelated feature has regressed.

In the handoff, report:

1.  What changed.
2.  Which files changed.
3.  Which existing features were preserved.
4.  Which checks were run and their actual results.
5.  Which checks were not run.
6.  Which assets are final, temporary, or missing.
7.  Any remaining blocker or decision.

Do not report an untested item as passed.

## 15. How Agents Must Work

Follow these rules on every task:

1.  Read this file before editing.
2.  Inspect the actual project before proposing a migration.
3.  State assumptions when important facts are missing.
4.  Ask a concise question if a missing fact blocks safe implementation.
5.  Prefer a small, reversible change over a broad rewrite.
6.  Preserve working features and user changes.
7.  Use real product data and supplied artwork.
8.  Do not invent business facts.
9.  Test the change.
10. Report results honestly.
11. Keep this context file updated when the user confirms a durable
    change to the brand, products, or technical architecture.
12. Do not rewrite this file for a one-off experiment unless asked.

## 16. Decision Filter

Before accepting a design or implementation choice, answer these
questions:

1.  Does it make the brand more recognisable?
2.  Does it show the real product more clearly?
3.  Does it support the customer's next action?
4.  Does it fit the black-and-lemon identity?
5.  Does the ice or 3D treatment add real depth rather than visual
    noise?
6.  Does it work on a small screen?
7.  Does it respect reduced motion and accessibility?
8.  Does it preserve existing functionality?
9.  Is the claim supported by real product data?
10. Can the implementation be tested and maintained?

If the answer to several questions is no, simplify the idea.

## 17. Final Creative Standard

The site should feel like a physical sticker and print universe brought
into a digital studio: black space, sharp lemon accents, expressive
type, real artwork, tactile depth, cold glossy highlights, and
controlled motion.

It should not feel like a dashboard, a generic Shopify theme, a crypto
landing page, or a template with a new logo.

**Make the products the stars. Make the interactions feel physical. Make
the buying path obvious. Make every effect earn its place. Make it
stick. Literally.**
